# The "Breakthrough" Problem: Why Some Phishing Kits Are Winning the Security Arms Race

In the ongoing battle between cybercriminals and corporate defense systems, a critical metric is emerging: the "breakthrough rate." This term, utilized by security researchers at Push Security, measures how often a phishing attempt successfully bypasses standard defenses—such as email security filters, web gateways, and safe browsing protocols—to land directly in an employee's inbox.

While organizations invest heavily in perimeter security, the reality is that not all phishing attacks are created equal. Some phishing kits are proving significantly more adept at slipping through these automated nets than others. For security teams, identifying these high-performing kits is no longer just a technical exercise; it is a necessity for survival.

### Why Breakthrough Rates Matter
The development of these sophisticated kits represents a shift in strategy for threat actors. Instead of relying on volume, attackers are increasingly focusing on efficacy. When a phishing kit achieves a high breakthrough rate, it effectively neutralizes the first line of defense that most companies rely on to protect their staff. 

When these controls fail, the burden of security shifts entirely to the end user. If an employee receives a malicious link that has already cleared the company’s automated security checks, they are far more likely to trust the communication, leading to credentials theft, malware installation, or unauthorized network access.

### Understanding the Threat Landscape
The core issue is that security controls are often reactive. They rely on identifying known threats or suspicious patterns. However, modern phishing kits are evolving to mimic legitimate traffic, use dynamic infrastructure, and employ obfuscation techniques that make them invisible to traditional scanners. 

For security professionals, the challenge is twofold:
1. **Visibility:** Teams must be able to track which specific kits are currently bypassing their filters.
2. **Adaptability:** Security protocols must be updated in real-time to counter the specific techniques these kits use to evade detection.

### What Readers Should Verify
While the concept of measuring breakthrough rates provides a clear framework for understanding current threats, readers should look for further information regarding:
* **Specific Indicators of Compromise (IOCs):** What are the common technical signatures associated with the kits currently showing the highest breakthrough rates?
* **Mitigation Strategies:** Beyond email filtering, what additional layers—such as identity-based security or browser isolation—are most effective at stopping these specific kits?

### Conclusion
As phishing kits become more sophisticated, the "breakthrough" has become the primary indicator of a successful attack. Organizations must move beyond static defenses and prioritize the analysis of which threats are actually reaching their users. By focusing on the kits that consistently evade automated controls, security teams can better prioritize their defenses and reduce the risk of a successful breach.
